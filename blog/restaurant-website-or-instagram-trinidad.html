Do Restaurants in Trinidad Need a Website or Just Instagram?

Every restaurant owner in Trinidad has heard the same advice: "You don't need a website, just post on Instagram." And honestly, that advice is half right. Instagram is where Trinis discover food. The problem is what happens after they discover you.

Let's walk through what each platform actually does for your restaurant, where Instagram quietly loses you money, and what a smart setup looks like in 2026 without overspending.

What Instagram does brilliantly

Credit where it is due. For a food business in T&T, Instagram is unbeatable at:

  • Discovery. Reels of doubles being made or oxtail falling off the bone travel fast. Nothing else puts your food in front of new eyes this cheaply.
  • Proof of life. Daily stories tell customers you are open, busy, and worth trying today.
  • Personality. Your vibe, your staff, your specials. This is where people fall in love with a spot before they ever visit.

If you only have energy for one thing this week, posting consistently on Instagram beats doing nothing. But here is where it stops working.

Where Instagram quietly costs you customers

1. Google cannot see your menu

When someone in Port of Spain searches "lunch near me" or "best roti in Chaguanas", Google shows websites and Google Business Profiles. Your Instagram page, with its 4,000 followers and gorgeous food photos, is practically invisible in those results. Every one of those searches is a hungry person with money in hand, and they are finding whoever has a real web presence.

2. Your menu is buried under last month's posts

A customer who wants to order has to scroll your feed hunting for a menu photo, hope it is current, and zoom in on a blurry image to read prices. Some give up and order from the place with the clear menu page instead. A website puts your full menu, prices, and hours one tap away, always current.

3. You do not control the algorithm

Reach on Instagram rises and falls with rules you cannot see. Accounts get hacked, disabled, or shadow-limited every day, and support is nearly impossible to contact. If your entire business lives on a platform you do not own, your business is one bad day away from starting over. Your website is yours. Nobody can take it down or throttle it.

4. Tourists and first-timers need more trust

Visitors planning a trip to Trinidad research online before they arrive. A restaurant with a clean website, visible menu, and Google reviews looks established. An Instagram-only presence reads as a food stall, even when your cooking is better than the hotel restaurant's.

The short version: Instagram gets you discovered. A website gets you chosen. They are not competitors, they are two halves of the same funnel.

What a restaurant website actually needs (and what it doesn't)

Restaurant websites fail when they try to do too much. You do not need a food blog, a newsletter popup, or a gallery with 200 photos. You need:

  • Your menu as a real page. Text Google can read, prices customers can see without zooming, updated in minutes when prices change.
  • Hours and location. With a map link that opens directions in one tap. Half your website visitors just want to know if you are open right now.
  • A WhatsApp or call button. This is how Trinidad orders food. Make it one tap from every page.
  • Five great photos. Your best dishes and your space. Quality over quantity.
  • Instagram links. The funnel works both ways. Website visitors become followers, followers become regulars.

Online ordering with payment integration is a genuine upgrade, but it is phase two. Get found and get the menu in front of people first.

  2. A fast website with your menu, hours, and WhatsApp button, so searches and link-in-bio clicks turn into orders.
